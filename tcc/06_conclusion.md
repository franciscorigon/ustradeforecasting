# 6 CONCLUSION

        This study compared ARIMA, Random Forest and LightGBM in forecasting 24 monthly bilateral trade series between the United States and Canada, Mexico and Brazil, structured by country, direction and sector. Point-forecast metrics, statistical significance tests and model-agnostic interpretability tools were combined within the six CRISP-DM phases.

        Random Forest emerged as the most accurate algorithm (MAPE 0.94%), followed by LightGBM (1.03%) and ARIMA (1.41%). The Diebold-Mariano test confirmed this hierarchy in 75% to 83% of the series. SHAP analysis identified bilateral exchange rates as a relevant predictor in both ML models: six-month moving averages in Random Forest, monthly percentage changes in LightGBM. Exchange rate fluctuations exert a measurable influence on monthly trade flow dynamics. Brazil registered the highest FX sensitivity, Canada the lowest and Mexico an intermediate position; commodities showed the greatest sectoral responsiveness, high-technology products the least.

        All four specific objectives were achieved. Sectoral FX vulnerability was mapped (objective a): commodities showed the highest FX sensitivity and high-technology products the lowest, consistently across the three country pairs. Error metrics and the Diebold-Mariano test evaluated the predictive power of each model (objective b), establishing the hierarchy RF > LightGBM > ARIMA. SHAP attribution ranked the WTI crude oil price, industrial production and exchange rate variables among the most influential predictors, addressing the effect of macroeconomic factors on trade flow volatility (objective c). Lag effects were examined through moving-average features (objective d), with SHAP revealing that Random Forest weighted medium-term six-month representations while LightGBM weighted short-term monthly changes.

        From an Information Systems perspective, the study delivered an end-to-end CRISP-DM implementation across 72 models, a dual-model SHAP analysis, pairwise statistical comparison via the Diebold-Mariano test with the Harvey et al. (1997) correction, and a versioned public repository. Taken together, these contributions extend the literature on predictive modeling of international trade by integrating algorithmic comparison, statistical rigor and interpretability within a single reproducible pipeline.

        Three limitations qualify these findings. Sectoral target series were estimated from annual HS2 proportions combined with monthly aggregate flows, an approximation that introduces systematic distortion, most pronounced in commodities. Covering January 2022 to December 2024, the test window overlaps with major macroeconomic disturbances (post-pandemic recovery, the 2022 energy price shock and U.S. monetary tightening), which may produce performance estimates not fully representative of more stable regimes. An asymmetric prediction protocol (one-step walk-forward for ARIMA versus 36-step direct for ML) was chosen to reflect the operational scenario of multi-month trade planning; it structurally favors ARIMA on shorter horizons and limits symmetric comparison.

        Four directions emerge for future research. Extending the modeling window backward to 2000–2009 would incorporate Global Financial Crisis dynamics, a period excluded from the current training set. Deep learning architectures such as LSTM and Transformer-based forecasters could serve as additional comparators. A recursive multi-step ML protocol with sequential retraining would enable a fully symmetric comparison with the ARIMA walk-forward design. Automating monthly data ingestion, scheduled retraining and forecast publication would produce a deployable system aligned with the Information Systems perspective.

# ABSTRACT

        This study assessed the predictive performance of Random Forest and LightGBM compared with ARIMA in forecasting bilateral trade flows between the United States and three trading partners (Canada, Mexico, and Brazil) from January 2010 to December 2024. The methodology followed the CRISP-DM process model across all six phases, integrating data from FRED, UN Comtrade, and the World Bank to construct 24 target series and 73 input features. The study trained 72 models, supplemented by a Naïve random-walk baseline. The evaluation combined error metrics (RMSE, MAE, MAPE), statistical significance tests (Friedman, Wilcoxon with Bonferroni, and Diebold-Mariano), and SHAP-based interpretability applied to both machine learning models. Random Forest achieved the lowest average error (MAPE 0.94%), followed by LightGBM (1.03%) and ARIMA (1.41%); the Diebold-Mariano test confirmed the predictive hierarchy in 75% to 83% of the series. SHAP analysis identified exchange rate variables among the most relevant predictors in both models. Brazil emerged as the most exchange-rate-sensitive partner and Canada the least, while commodities showed greater responsiveness than high-technology products. The study published the full pipeline in a public repository for reproducibility.

**Keywords:** exchange rate forecasting; machine learning; ARIMA; bilateral trade flows; SHAP.

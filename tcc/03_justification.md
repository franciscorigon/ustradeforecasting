# 3 JUSTIFICATION

        Exchange rate volatility affects international competitiveness, investment decisions, and resource allocation in the world market (RAHMAN et al., 2025). Detailing how exchange rate changes translate into trade flow changes is crucial for economic policies and strategic decisions of exporter and importer companies (KIM; LE, 2024).

        Accurate predictions from modern machine learning algorithms, compared with traditional econometric methods, are highly relevant for cost minimization and profit maximization. This accuracy is an essential asset for nations and companies in international markets, helping them adapt faster to global changes (ABIR et al., 2024).

        Beyond its practical contributions, this work is methodologically relevant for implementing Data Science best practices (ETL, experiment tracking) to ensure reproducibility of experiments and results. As emphasized by Vranješ and Niggemann (2024, p. 1–2):

> the iterative process of training, testing, and validating ML models requires empirical evidence to ensure their accuracy and reliability in real-world applications, making experimentation a cornerstone of the ML research methodology, which reinforces the relevance of structuring, documenting, and monitoring the experimental workflow adopted in this study.

        The paper thus extends the literature on predictive modeling of international trade, offering a framework to assess how trade flows adjust to exchange rate and macroeconomic shocks.

        The empirical results in Section 4 confirm this relevance: ML models outperformed the linear baseline in most of the 24 series, and SHAP analysis exposed heterogeneous transmission of exchange rate shocks across countries and sectors. The complete pipeline was published for reproducibility.

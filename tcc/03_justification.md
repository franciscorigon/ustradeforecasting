# 3 JUSTIFICATION

        Exchange rate volatility is a central factor that affects international competitiveness, investment decisions, and the allocation of resources in the global market (RAHMAN et al., 2025). Understanding how exchange rate movements translate into changes in trade flows is therefore essential for the design of effective economic policies and for supporting the strategic decisions of exporting and importing firms (KIM; LE, 2024). The systematic comparison of forecasting methods conducted in this study contributes to this understanding by quantifying the predictive role of currency movements at the level of bilateral country pairs and sectoral aggregates.

        The accuracy with which these dynamics can be predicted using modern machine learning algorithms — when contrasted with traditional econometric methods and with the information technology infrastructure that supports them — has become increasingly relevant for cost minimization and profit maximization. This accuracy constitutes an essential asset for nations and firms operating in international markets, supporting their capacity to adapt rapidly to changes in the global economy (ABIR et al., 2024). The empirical evidence presented in Section 6 confirms this practical relevance: the machine learning models outperformed the linear baseline in the majority of the 24 series, and the SHAP-based interpretability analysis exposed the heterogeneous transmission of exchange rate shocks across countries and sectors.

        Beyond its practical contributions, this work is methodologically relevant from the perspective of Information Systems, as it relies on the implementation of Data Science best practices — including a structured ETL process, hyperparameter optimization with experiment tracking, and model interpretability tools — to enforce the reproducibility of modeling experiments and, by extension, of the results. As emphasized by Vranješ and Niggemann (2024, p. 1–2):

> the iterative process of training, testing, and validating ML models requires empirical evidence to ensure their accuracy and reliability in real-world applications, making experimentation a cornerstone of the ML research methodology, which reinforces the relevance of structuring, documenting, and monitoring the experimental workflow adopted in this study.

        The integration of these methodological commitments — CRISP-DM as a guiding process model, automated hyperparameter optimization with Optuna, statistically grounded comparison through the Diebold-Mariano test, and SHAP-based interpretability — extends the existing literature on the predictive modeling of international trade and provides a reference framework for examining how trade flows adjust to exchange rate and macroeconomic disturbances. The complete pipeline, including processed datasets, trained model objects, and evaluation notebooks, was published in a versioned public repository, ensuring that the empirical results can be independently verified and extended by future research.

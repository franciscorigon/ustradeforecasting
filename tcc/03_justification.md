# 3 JUSTIFICATION

        Exchange rate volatility is a key factor that in fact affects international competitiveness, investment decisions, and resource allocation in the world market (RAHMAN et al., 2025). Detailing how exchange rate changes translate into trade flow changes is therefore crucial to designing efficient economic policies and supporting strategic decisions for exporter and importer companies (KIM; LE, 2024).

        Accuracy in predicting these parameters with the use of modern machine learning algorithms, when compared to the traditional econometric methods and IT tools developed upon these solutions, become highly relevant as going from minimizing costs over maximizing profits. This accuracy is an essential asset for nations and companies active on the international markets by helping them adapt faster to those changes encountered at a global level (ABIR et al., 2024).

        Moreover, beyond its practical contributions, this work is relevant from a methodological point of view, as it relies on the implementation of Data Science best practices, such as ETL process and experiment tracking, to enforce the reproducibility of modeling experiments, and therefore results, in a transparent and consistent manner, thus promoting scientific validity. As emphasized by Vranješ and Niggemann (2024, p. 1–2):

> the iterative process of training, testing, and validating ML models requires empirical evidence to ensure their accuracy and reliability in real-world applications, making experimentation a cornerstone of the ML research methodology, which reinforces the relevance of structuring, documenting, and monitoring the experimental workflow adopted in this study.

        In this way, the paper also extends the literature on predictive modeling of international trade, representing a reference framework to scrutinize the ability of trade flows to adjust to exchange rate and macroeconomic interferences.

        The empirical results presented in Section 6 confirm this practical relevance: the machine learning models outperformed the linear baseline in the majority of the 24 series, and the SHAP-based interpretability analysis exposed the heterogeneous transmission of exchange rate shocks across countries and sectors. The complete pipeline was published in a versioned public repository, ensuring reproducibility and supporting independent verification by future research.

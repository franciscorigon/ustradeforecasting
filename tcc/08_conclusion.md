# 8 CONCLUSION

        This study assessed the relative predictive performance of Random Forest and LightGBM models with respect to the ARIMA model in forecasting bilateral trade flows between the United States and three major partners — Canada, Mexico, and Brazil — across 24 monthly series structured by country, direction, and sector. The comparison was addressed through point-forecast metrics, statistical significance tests, and model-agnostic interpretability tools, organized within the six phases of the CRISP-DM process model.

        The main empirical findings are as follows. Random Forest emerged as the most accurate algorithm overall, with a mean MAPE of 0.94% across the 24 series, followed by LightGBM (1.03%) and ARIMA (1.41%). The Diebold-Mariano test confirmed the predictive hierarchy with statistical significance in 75% to 83% of the series across the three pairwise comparisons. SHAP analysis applied to both machine learning models identified the bilateral exchange rate as a relevant predictor — through six-month moving averages in Random Forest and through monthly percentage changes in LightGBM — confirming the central hypothesis that exchange rate fluctuations exert a measurable influence on monthly trade flow dynamics. This finding should be interpreted as a positive answer to the primary research question, with the qualification that the exchange rate operates as one relevant macroeconomic driver among others (notably energy prices and industrial production) rather than as a dominant single factor — a moderation consistent with the intrinsic characteristics of the variable, including its short-term volatility and its function as a price relative to other aggregates. At the country level, Brazil exhibited the highest exchange rate sensitivity, Canada the lowest, and Mexico an intermediate position consistent with its hybrid integration profile under the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement. At the sectoral level, commodities displayed the greatest responsiveness to currency movements, while high-technology products were the least sensitive across all three country pairs.

        From the perspective of Information Systems, the study delivered an end-to-end CRISP-DM implementation with 72 trained models, a dual-model SHAP analysis that reduces interpretive dependence on a single algorithmic family, statistically grounded model comparison through the Diebold-Mariano test with the Harvey et al. (1997) correction, and a fully versioned public repository that supports reproducibility as a first-class deliverable. These methodological contributions extend the literature on the predictive modeling of international trade by integrating systematic algorithmic comparison, statistical rigor, and interpretability tools within a single pipeline.

        Several limitations apply. The sectoral target series were estimated from annual HS2 proportions combined with monthly aggregate flows, an approximation that introduces systematic distortion most pronounced in the Commodities category. The non-significant Friedman result reflects the limited statistical power of the test under the sample size of 24 series; the Diebold-Mariano test, applied to the underlying error sequences, recovered the predictive hierarchy with statistical significance and constituted the primary instrument for ranking the three algorithms. The asymmetric prediction protocol — one-step walk-forward for ARIMA versus 36-step direct for the machine learning models — structurally favors ARIMA on shorter horizons and the Naïve baseline on the immediate-step comparison.

        Several directions for future research emerge from these findings and limitations. The modeling window can be extended backward to 2000–2009 to incorporate post-Global Financial Crisis dynamics into the training data, an extension previously identified as desirable in the supervisory dialogue. Deep learning architectures such as Long Short-Term Memory networks and Transformer-based forecasters could be incorporated as additional comparators, complementing the tree-based ensembles employed here. A recursive multi-step machine learning protocol with sequential retraining could be implemented to provide a fully symmetric comparison with the ARIMA walk-forward design, removing the protocol asymmetry identified in this study. The asymmetric transmission of currency appreciation versus depreciation across exports and imports remains a relevant question that the current dataset and pipeline can support with further analytical work. Finally, the operational extension of the pipeline — automated monthly data ingestion, scheduled retraining, and forecast publication — would represent a concrete deployment artifact aligned with the Information Systems perspective adopted in this study.

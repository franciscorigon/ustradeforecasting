# 8 CONCLUSION

        This study assessed the predictive performance of Random Forest and LightGBM compared with ARIMA in forecasting bilateral trade flows between the United States and three partners (Canada, Mexico and Brazil) across 24 monthly series structured by country, direction and sector. The comparison combined point-forecast metrics, statistical significance tests and model-agnostic interpretability tools, organized within the six CRISP-DM phases.

        Random Forest emerged as the most accurate algorithm (MAPE 0.94% across the 24 series), followed by LightGBM (1.03%) and ARIMA (1.41%). The Diebold-Mariano test confirmed this hierarchy with statistical significance in 75% to 83% of the series. SHAP analysis identified the bilateral exchange rate as a relevant predictor in both ML models, through six-month moving averages in Random Forest and monthly percentage changes in LightGBM, confirming that exchange rate fluctuations exert a measurable influence on monthly trade flow dynamics. This positively answers the primary research question. At the country level, Brazil had the highest sensitivity, Canada the lowest, and Mexico an intermediate position under USMCA. At the sectoral level, commodities had the greatest responsiveness, while high-technology products were the least sensitive.

        These findings confirm that the specific objectives were achieved. The sectors most vulnerable to exchange rate changes were identified (objective a): commodities showed the highest FX sensitivity and high-technology products the lowest, consistently across the three country pairs. The impact across time lags was examined through the lag and moving-average feature structure (objective b), with SHAP revealing that Random Forest weighted medium-term six-month representations while LightGBM weighted short-term monthly changes. The predictive power of each model was evaluated through error metrics and the Diebold-Mariano test (objective c), establishing the hierarchy RF > LightGBM > ARIMA. Finally, the effect of macroeconomic factors on trade flow volatility was examined through SHAP attribution (objective d), which ranked the WTI crude oil price, industrial production and exchange rate variables among the most influential predictors.

        From an Information Systems perspective, the study delivered an end-to-end CRISP-DM implementation with 72 trained models, a dual-model SHAP analysis that reduces interpretive dependence on a single algorithm, statistically grounded comparison through the Diebold-Mariano test with the Harvey et al. (1997) correction, and a versioned public repository for reproducibility. These contributions extend the literature on predictive modeling of international trade by integrating algorithmic comparison, statistical rigor and interpretability within a single pipeline.

        Three methodological limitations apply. The sectoral target series were estimated from annual HS2 proportions combined with monthly aggregate flows, an approximation that introduces systematic distortion, most pronounced in commodities. The test window (January 2022 to December 2024) overlaps with major macroeconomic disturbances — post-pandemic recovery, the 2022 energy price shock and U.S. monetary tightening — which may produce performance estimates not fully representative of more stable regimes. The asymmetric prediction protocol (one-step walk-forward for ARIMA versus 36-step direct for ML) was chosen to reflect the operational scenario of multi-month trade planning; it structurally favors ARIMA on shorter horizons and limits symmetric comparison with the ML models.

        Several directions for future research emerge from these findings. The modeling window can be extended backward to 2000–2009 to incorporate post-Global Financial Crisis dynamics, an extension previously identified as desirable. Deep learning architectures such as LSTM and Transformer-based forecasters could be incorporated as additional comparators. A recursive multi-step ML protocol with sequential retraining could provide a fully symmetric comparison with the ARIMA walk-forward design, removing the protocol asymmetry. The asymmetric transmission of currency appreciation versus depreciation across exports and imports remains a relevant question the current dataset can support. Finally, the operational extension of the pipeline (automated monthly data ingestion, scheduled retraining and forecast publication) would represent a concrete deployment artifact aligned with the Information Systems perspective.

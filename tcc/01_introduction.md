# 1 INTRODUCTION

        This study investigated the impact of exchange rate volatility on bilateral trade flows, with particular emphasis on the trading relationship between the United States and three of its main partners — Canada, Mexico, and Brazil — the latter being its largest trading partner in Latin America (USITC, 2025). The motivation for this comparative design is the heterogeneous institutional and structural relationship between the United States and each of the three economies. Canada and Mexico trade with the United States under the institutional framework of the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA); Brazil, in contrast, has historically conducted its bilateral trade with the United States outside any comprehensive free trade agreement of equivalent scope (USTR, 2020). The contrast between an advanced and a developing economy enables the identification of nuances and asymmetries in the way exchange rate fluctuations are transmitted to trade flows, contributing to an already well-established debate (AUBOIN; RUTA, 2011).

        The analytical period was originally framed for the years 2000 to 2025, combining historical data and forecasts to identify trends and estimate future effects. Operational constraints imposed by the data sources — particularly the availability of monthly bilateral trade data in the United Nations Comtrade database — restricted the modeling window to the years 2010 to 2024, comprising 180 monthly observations. The choice of monthly granularity was made to accommodate the volatility of short-term trade flows and exchange rate movements (SHEN et al., 2021). Beyond an aggregate trade perspective, the analysis was conducted at sectoral granularity, allowing the assessment of why different categories of goods may respond asymmetrically to exchange rate fluctuations and offering a finer characterization of the heterogeneity of effects across sectors.

        On the methodological side, the study adopted state-of-the-art machine learning models — Random Forest and LightGBM — to forecast the bilateral trade flows and compared their predictive accuracy with the traditional ARIMA model (SONG; CHEN, 2024). The exports and imports of goods between the United States and each of the three partners were treated as the response variables, while macroeconomic indicators — including nominal and real exchange rates, indices of industrial production, the Consumer Price Index, and the WTI crude oil price — were treated as explanatory inputs (KALLIANIOTIS, 2022). Trade flows and exchange rate dynamics can be substantially affected by extreme events such as global financial crises and pandemics, and the influence of these high-volatility regimes on the predictive performance of the models was examined accordingly (BATARSEH et al., 2020). The entire research process followed the Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining (CRISP-DM), from Business Understanding through Deployment (MARTÍNEZ‐PLUMED et al., 2019).

        The primary research question that guided this work was the following: what is the impact of exchange rate variations on the export and import flows between the United States and Canada, Mexico, and Brazil, and which forecasting models are best suited for producing such forecasts? Three subsidiary questions complemented the primary one: at which time horizons is the predictive power most informative, and how does it vary across models; which industries are most sensitive to exchange rate changes, and how does this sensitivity differ between developed and emerging economies; and how do exports and imports respond asymmetrically to currency appreciation and depreciation. The comparative design — three algorithms, twenty-four bilateral series, three country pairs, four sectoral aggregates — was constructed to address these questions in a structured manner.

        The empirical results obtained from the analysis are reported in Section 6 and interpreted in Section 7. In summary, Random Forest and LightGBM outperformed ARIMA in 75% and 83% of the 24 series respectively, as confirmed by the Diebold-Mariano test, with Random Forest emerging as the best-performing algorithm. The SHAP analysis identified the bilateral exchange rate as a structurally relevant predictor in both machine learning models, addressing the primary research question affirmatively. Brazil emerged as the most exchange-rate-sensitive trading partner, while Canada exhibited the lowest sensitivity, and commodity flows displayed greater responsiveness to exchange rate movements than high-technology products across all three country pairs.

        The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the general and specific objectives that guided the study. Section 3 outlines the justification and methodological relevance of the work. Section 4 reviews the theoretical framework, covering the economic concepts of exchange rate, trade flows, and sectoral sensitivity, as well as the time series and machine learning models employed. Section 5 describes the methodology, organized along the six phases of the CRISP-DM process model. Section 6 presents the empirical results, including the model comparison, the statistical significance tests, and the SHAP-based interpretability analysis. Section 7 discusses the findings in light of the research questions and the existing literature, identifies the methodological contributions of the study, and acknowledges its limitations. Section 8 concludes and outlines directions for future research.

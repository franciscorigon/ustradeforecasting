# 1 INTRODUCTION

        The purpose of this study was to investigate the impact of exchange rate volatility on bilateral trade flows, with particular emphasis on the trading relationship between the U.S. and its main partners, Canada, Mexico and Brazil, which is its largest partner in Latin America (USITC, 2025).

        Canada and Mexico trade with the USA through USMCA (United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement). However, Brazil trades extensively without a full-fledged free trading agreement like that (USTR, 2020). The comparison between an advanced and a developing economy enabled the detection of nuances or differences on the pattern of sensitiveness that foreign trade demonstrates when reproducing exchange rate fluctuations, contributing to enhancing the already well-established debate (AUBOIN; RUTA, 2011).

        The period analyzed and tested was originally framed from 2000 to 2025, with the actual modeling window restricted to 2010 to 2024 due to monthly data availability in the United Nations Comtrade database, combining historical data and forecasts in the determination of trends and estimations of future effects. This study also accommodated fine-grained data at monthly time slices, so as to capture the changes of volatile unpredictable trade flows and exchange rate movements (SHEN et al., 2021).

        Apart from an aggregate trade perspective, the study also offered sectoral analysis to ascertain why different commodities and services may respond asymmetrically to exchange rate fluctuations, offering a better understanding of the heterogeneity of effects among sectors.

        The study used state-of-the-art machine learning models (Random Forests, LightGBM) to forecast the effect of such fluctuations and compared them to traditional statistical methods such as ARIMA in terms of predictive ability (SONG; CHEN, 2024). It included in its analysis the exports and imports of goods between the U.S. and the chosen countries, using as explanatory independent variables, macroeconomic indicators such as GDP, nominal and real exchange rates, volume indices of production, and inflation (KALLIANIOTIS, 2022). In addition, trade relationships and the exchange rate dynamics can be seriously affected by outlier events (such as global financial crises and pandemics), therefore the influence of such extreme observations on predictive models was examined to see how well they hold up in these high-volatility instances due to nonlinear relationships (BATARSEH et al., 2020). This research followed a structured approach according to CRISP-DM, from business understanding to deployment of the model (MARTÍNEZ‐PLUMED et al., 2019).

        The primary question to be answered was: "What is the impact of exchange rate variations on export and import flows between the United States and Canada, Mexico, and Brazil, and which forecasting models are best for making these forecasts?"

        Additional questions were also addressed regarding: What are the horizons where predictive power is most informative for assessing the impact of exchange rate movements on international trade, and how does this predictive power vary across different models? Which industries are more sensitive with respect to exchange rate changes and how does this vary between developed countries versus emerging economies? In addition, this study endeavors to explore the asymmetric effects on exports and imports in reaction to currency appreciation and depreciation with a view to finding differences in behavior. The study also examined the effect of the depreciation of the dollar on bilateral trade balances, looking at how this type of monetary adjustment affects American export prices in foreign markets as well as import prices for American consumers. Moreover, it compared the forecasting ability of traditional econometric models with that of machine learning models in forecasting non-stationary or strongly fluctuating time series.

        The empirical results obtained from the analysis are reported in Section 6 and interpreted in Section 7. In summary, Random Forest and LightGBM outperformed ARIMA in 75% and 83% of the 24 series respectively, as confirmed by the Diebold-Mariano test, with Random Forest emerging as the best-performing algorithm. The SHAP analysis identified the bilateral exchange rate as a relevant predictor in both machine learning models, addressing the primary research question. Brazil emerged as the most exchange-rate-sensitive trading partner, while Canada exhibited the lowest sensitivity, and commodity flows displayed greater responsiveness to exchange rate movements than high-technology products across all three country pairs.

        The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the general and specific objectives that guided the study. Section 3 outlines the justification of the work. Section 4 reviews the theoretical framework, covering the economic concepts of exchange rate, trade flows, and sectoral sensitivity, as well as the time series and machine learning models employed. Section 7 discusses the findings in light of the research questions and the existing literature. Section 8 concludes and outlines directions for future research.
